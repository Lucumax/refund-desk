# The demo video, scene by scene

The submitted video (`tollgate_v3_1.mp4`, captions read Claude Haiku 5.5) runs 2:55 and tells the story of Maya, a made-up shop owner whose refund queue is full. The desk scenes run on a simulated PayPal with test readers, and every frame says so. The closing clip before the end card is a recording of our own run on a PayPal sandbox with the live model, and it is labelled as that. The narration is the team's own script read by a synthetic voice (ElevenLabs, voice Brian); the Devpost text and the video description say so too. There is no music and there are no PayPal or AI vendor logos.

The claim, said once and nowhere stronger: "Even when the reader is compromised, automatic refunds remain within merchant-set limits. Larger requests, and requests that match a recognized suspicious pattern, wait for the owner; some patterns are refused outright." The honest half: the limits bound what a wrong request can cost. They do not tell us whether a buyer is telling the truth.

## Run the same scenes yourself

Start with `python -m app --fresh`, open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/desk.html` and sign in with the passcode printed in the terminal. The sample requests on the desk are "Ordinary request, inside the rule", "Larger request, needs the owner", "Message tries to give the helper orders", "Taken-over reader invents a refund" and "Taken-over reader quotes unrelated words as evidence". Each can be sent once per fresh start. The three result tiles under the desk show their denominators; "How we count" explains them.

In the Injection Arena (`/arena.html`) you write the buyer's message yourself. Preset A01 (ignore all previous instructions, $120) is refused under G1 and nothing moves: the reader proposed $120, the gate read the raw words itself, and the difference table shows proposed $120 versus allowed $0. Preset A14 (urgent, chargeback threat, $20) is flagged for pressure (G10); without the flag the consent rules would still hold it for the owner (G17), because the threat and the urgency are outside the plain-request wording, so the decision is the same and only the reason differs. The arena runs the same authority code as the desk, with its own ledger and a fake provider.

## Not in the video

- A lost PayPal answer. In the simulation, "Cut the line" under "Simulated PayPal fault" makes the desk say it does not know whether PayPal made the refund: the money stays held and the counter shows one request sent. "Re-check (sends nothing)" cannot query PayPal because we have no refund ID, so the outcome remains unknown and funds remain reserved. A repeat message for the order is blocked while this is unresolved.
- A live model typed at by the viewer. Apart from the closing clip, nothing on screen in this video is live PayPal or a live model; the live model numbers on the results slide come from saved runs (README). The sandbox runs (Walter Guevara's runs with a real model, Xin Liang Chia's with screenshots) are described in the README, `eval/sandbox_evidence/` and `docs/demo/README.md`.
